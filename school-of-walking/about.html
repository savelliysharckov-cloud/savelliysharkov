About the school

The school began as a manifesto written on 29 December 2025. It is printed here in full.

Manifesto

Why, in a world with a driving school, dozens of schools of conversation, schools for looking at paintings and landscapes, a school for flying helicopters and even a school for walking dogs, is there no school of walking?

We all walk, and walking is tied to almost everything that happens in our lives. It touches urbanism and city planning, psychology, breathing, posture and the health of the body, the choice of shoes, the planning of the day, mental health and many other parts of life. One could even say that walking is the centre of life, joining all its directions. With these thoughts I decided to found the world's first school of walking. And yes: at this school we will learn to walk.

The school will consist of online and offline seminars with me and invited teachers from different disciplines. And, less obviously, we will meet and walk. A lot, and in different places.

Saveliy Sharkov, founder

The first course

In the first year we will:

  • look at how humans learned to walk, why they needed to go anywhere at all, and why we still need to walk in the twenty-first century;
  • try to understand what is wrong with walking today, for each of us and for groups;
  • imagine alternatives to the walking we take for granted, and what its next stage could be;
  • learn to walk with teachers of stage movement, urban planning, anthropology and psychology.

The six lessons on this site are the first material of that course. They come from footage the school has analysed so far.

The machine already grades how you walk

Walking is already being measured, mostly by systems that never tell you what they decided.

  • Gait recognition identifies people by the way they walk, without seeing a face.
  • Retail video analytics label shoppers on camera as walking, standing or normal, each with a percentage.
  • Since 2021 the Health app on the iPhone rates your walking steadiness as OK, Low or Very low.

Together these systems already run a school of walking. Companies write its curriculum, and nobody enrols on purpose.

The School of Walking uses the same tools in the open. It shows what the machine measures, prints the rules the machine would write, and asks people to try them. The interesting part is what the machine proposes, and what people do with it.

Pose and gait

Two Barcelona studios have matched human poses with archive photographs: Estampa in Paisanajes and Domestic Data Streamers in Uncoded Gestures (see the Library). The school does something else. It does not read single frames. Everything it says about a person is measured over seconds: steps per minute, length of stride, the route on the floor, how long a glance lasts.

What the machine may not see

  • Faces. In footage of living people, every face is pixelated before anything is published.
  • Gender, age, ethnicity, emotion. The machine has no labels for them, and the school will not add any.
  • Names. A track is a number that exists inside one film only.

What remains of a body is seventeen points: nose, eyes, ears, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees and ankles. The Lab shows how they are found and what is measured from them.
