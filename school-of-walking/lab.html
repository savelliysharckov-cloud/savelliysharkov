Lab

How the school sees: what the machine finds in a frame, what it measures over time, and what it is not allowed to see. Below that, the journal of what was tried and what was dropped.

From footage to figure

  1. Find the bodies

    A pose model (Ultralytics YOLO26) finds every person in every frame and seventeen points on each body.

  2. Follow them

    ByteTrack links each body from frame to frame. Short gaps are filled; tracks that jump are cut.

  3. Hold the camera still

    For hand-held footage, the background is followed with optical flow and its motion is subtracted. People are masked out so they do not move the floor.

  4. Hide the faces

    In footage of living people a second detector (MTCNN) finds the faces the pose model missed, and every face is pixelated.

  5. Measure

    Body height is the ruler. From it come steps, tempo, stride, speed, head rays, looks, sips and routes.

  6. Draw

    The machine's view is drawn at twice the size and scaled down: graphite on paper, or white on black.

Measures

Step
The moment the ankles are furthest apart. The distance between them is measured in body heights, so the left-right mix-ups of the model do not matter.
Tempo
Steps per minute, from the time between steps. Needs at least three steps in one track.
Stride
Distance per step, in body heights. Speed divided by tempo.
Speed
Body heights per second along the walk. One height is about 1.7 m; plates that say m/s use torso length (0.51 m) or shoulder width (0.40 m) as the ruler.
Head ray
A line from the middle of the ears through the nose. A face seen straight on gets a small circle instead.
Look
A head ray that lands on another head of similar size (a ratio between 0.6 and 1.67, so the two are at about the same depth) for at least 0.3 s.
Sip
A wrist within 1.25 head widths of the mouth for at least 0.2 s.
Route
The point on the floor under a body, taken from the ankles, or the hips or shoulders when the feet are hidden. A dot every 22 cm and a tick every metre, drawn only while the body moves faster than 0.3 m/s.
Track
One body followed from frame to frame. Gaps up to 0.2 s are filled; a track is cut where it jumps.

Journal

  • October 2026

    First skeleton tests

    Street, winter crossing and a 1928 film, each reduced to skeletons with a trail of past steps. Only the machine's side is shown here.

  • October 2026

    Speed as colour

    Dropped

    Ljubljana, Stari trg. Skeletons coloured by speed. It looked good and explained nothing, so the school stopped colouring and started measuring.

  • October 2026

    Counterflow, first pass

    Rennes, INRIA. Sixty people in a ring corridor, two directions. The first pass coloured the directions; the plate that came out of it is in Lesson 3.

  • October 2026

    First plates, in Russian

    Redrawn

    Score of speeds, a street plan rebuilt from steps, the rhythm of a step, four streets compared. Red marked speed. The plates were redrawn in English and without red; the comparison of four streets became Plate 6.

  • October 2026

    Wallpapers

    A designer friend at the university suggested wallpaper and said he would buy it. The first repeats were crowd, pause and procession; the second set, crowd, procession and traces, moved towards a machine look.

  • October 2026

    Indoors

    From the street into venues, where the wallpaper could hang. Ten ways of drawing a room were proposed; three stayed: where each head points, the route across the floor, and the sips.

  • October 2026

    Venue films and wallpapers

    Diptychs of a 1946 restaurant, an ice-cream shop and a Japanese restaurant; wallpapers of glances, routes and sips, tried in eight rooms. Then a parade from 1944, white on black.

  • November 2026

    Neighbours, Plate 6 and this site

    A look at two Barcelona neighbours, Estampa and Domestic Data Streamers, settled the school's line: gait, not pose. Then the atlases were redrawn in English, Plate 6 was measured, and the site went up.
