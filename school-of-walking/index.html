School
of Walking

Toronto (1928). A street of passers-by, replayed from their skeletons: seventeen points per body, a dotted route under each one, a ring where a foot lands.

There is a school for driving, a school for flying helicopters, even a school for walking dogs. Why is there no school of walking?

Everyone walks, and walking touches almost everything in a life: the city and how it is planned, breathing, posture, shoes, the shape of the day, the state of the mind.

The School of Walking is a school and a study at the same time. A machine watches people walk in old films, cafés and streets. It measures what it sees and writes rules from the numbers. The school publishes the rules, so that people can test them with their own feet and argue with them.

Lessons

Six lessons for the first course: rhythm, speed, counterflow, the room, formation, cinema.

Observatory

Four films, each seen twice: as footage, and as everything the machine kept of it.

Entrance exam

Tap your steps for twenty seconds. Find out whose walk in the archive you share.

Pose is a noun.
Gait is a verb.

Most machines that look at bodies freeze them: one frame, one pose, one label. The school never reads a single frame. Everything it says about a person is measured over time: the beat of the steps, the length of the stride, the route across the floor, how long a glance lasts.
