History

This format is over 150 years old.

Sheets with patterns for dozens of garments printed on top of one another appeared in the mid-19th century.

Similar inserts could be found in magazines such as Der Bazar (Berlin, from 1854), Harper's Bazar (New York, from 1867) and El Salón de la Moda (Barcelona, 1884–1916). The ones shown here come from two Soviet magazines: Zhurnal Mod (Moscow) and Rigas Modes (Riga).

The pieces were overlapped to fit as many as possible onto a single sheet.

Tracing

Click a lightbox to look closer

To trace a single piece, the sheet was placed against a lightbox. But more commonly a sunlit window was used. Then it was traced through tracing paper.

Prints are on both sides, so the light revealed both sides at once. The lines of one side overlapped with the lines of the other, and finding a single piece among them was not easy.

Another way to copy was tracing paper laid over the sheet. A tracing wheel was then rolled along a line, making holes.
